01How China orders fail

Three failures that can be found before money moves

Each has a check that can be run before money moves.

The supplier

The supplier calls itself a factory. Its licence shows a trading company, or a company name that differs from the one on the invoice. You find out after a 30% deposit has been paid. The Counterparty Check compares the registration, the licence scope and the invoice details before any deposit is sent.

The contract

The contract is a one-page invoice in English, with no governing law stated and no quality annex. When the goods arrive wrong there is nothing specific to enforce. The Transaction File sets out supply terms with a specification and quality annex, and links payment of the balance to a passed inspection.

The border

Electrical goods reach Durban without an NRCS Letter of Authority, or the invoice, packing list and tariff code disagree. The consignment is held while SARS raises queries and storage charges accrue each day. The destination rules and the documents are checked against each other before shipment.

03Regulatory update, South Africa

A Certificate of Conformity is now required for nine categories of Chinese goods

Under the Pre-Export Verification of Conformity (PVoC) programme, Phase 1 applies to imports from China. The Border Management Authority and SARS Customs may refuse entry to covered goods that arrive without a valid certificate to South African National Standards.

  • 01Cosmetics and toiletries
  • 02Cookware and kitchenware
  • 03Furniture
  • 04Bicycles and sports gear
  • 05Plastic toys
  • 06Generators
  • 07Gas stoves
  • 08Plumbing supplies
  • 09Construction materials
Basis
Government Gazette No. 54374, published 20 March 2026
Applies to
Phase 1: imports from the People’s Republic of China
Certificate
A Certificate of Conformity to applicable South African National Standards, issued by a conformity assessment body appointed by the South African Bureau of Standards. For Phase 1 the programme lists CCIC, SGS and Intertek. Meridian does not issue certificates.
If missing
The shipment may be returned to the country of origin or destroyed at the importer’s expense. The programme publishes no penalty schedule.
Meridian’s part
The Import Compliance Assessment establishes whether your product is covered and what the supplier must provide before you order.

Checked against the programme site, pvoc.co.za, on 29 September 2026. Scope may change in later phases, so confirm it before you order.
