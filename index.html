01How China orders fail

Three failures that can be found before money moves

Each can be found with a Meridian check before the deposit is paid.

The supplier

The supplier calls itself a factory. Its licence shows a trading company, or the names on the contract, the invoice and the bank account belong to three different companies. You find out after a 30% deposit has been paid. The Supplier Check compares the registration, the licence scope and every name on the paperwork before any deposit is sent.

The product

The supplier sends a test report for a similar model, or from a laboratory the NRCS does not accept. Mains-powered appliances, plugs, adaptors, lamps and luminaires need an NRCS Letter of Authority before they can be sold in South Africa, and Wi-Fi and Bluetooth devices also need ICASA type approval. The Import Compliance Assessment names the compulsory specification that applies and lists the documents the supplier must provide for your model.

The border

NRCS inspectors find the product does not comply, or the importer named on the shipping documents is not the holder of the Letter of Authority. The consignment is held, and non-compliant goods can be returned to the country of origin or destroyed. In 2024/25 the NRCS reported four containers of paraffin heaters destroyed at Durban because the importer’s name differed from the one on the Letter of Authority. The Pre-Order Check matches these names before you order.

30 September 2026. South Africa

South Africa’s PVoC programme is on hold

The Pre-Export Verification of Conformity (PVoC) programme was published in Government Gazette No. 54374 on 20 March 2026, for listed goods from China, with a six-month transition. In June 2026 the SABS placed all implementation activity on hold, pending a consultation process. As at 30 September 2026 we have found no announcement that it has resumed.

The Gazette states that the programme applies only to products that are not subject to compulsory specifications administered by the NRCS. Electrical goods regulated by the NRCS continue to need a Letter of Authority, whatever happens to PVoC.

Basis
Government Gazette No. 54374, 20 March 2026.
Status
On hold since June 2026. No restart date announced.
Scope
Products outside NRCS compulsory specifications. The product list has been reported inconsistently, so confirm it against the Gazette before relying on it.
Meridian’s part
The Import Compliance Assessment establishes whether a product falls under an NRCS compulsory specification, PVoC, or neither.

Sources: Government Gazette No. 54374, Freight News and Ziegler. Checked on 30 September 2026.

Sample. Invented example for illustration. Not a client matter.

Question
Can a Johannesburg wholesaler import 2,000 Wi-Fi smart plugs from a Shenzhen supplier and sell them in South Africa?
Rules
A smart plug adaptor falls to be assessed under VC 8008 (plugs, socket-outlets and socket-outlet adaptors), subject to confirmation against the product’s configuration. As a Wi-Fi device it also needs ICASA type approval. If the wholesaler runs the companion app or cloud account under its own brand, it is responsible for customer data sent to servers outside South Africa, and POPIA section 72 applies to that transfer.
Conclusion
The order should not proceed on the documents supplied. The supplier’s test report names a different model number, and no ICASA approval has been provided. Confidence: high on the test report point, medium on the specification pending the product sample.
Actions
Request a test report for the ordered model from an accepted laboratory. Request the ICASA approval or the radio test data needed to apply. Confirm who operates the app and where its servers are located.
